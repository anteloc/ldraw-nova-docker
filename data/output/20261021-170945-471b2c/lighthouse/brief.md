# Sideways-stud lighthouse: design brief

## Identity
A working coastal lighthouse: a tapering tower on a rock plinth, lit from a glazed lantern room at the top. It should read as a lighthouse at thumbnail size, with the lantern as the single focal point.

## Silhouette
- Wide rock base (about 10 studs across).
- Tall tower tapering from about 8 studs across at the base to about 6 at the top.
- Gallery ring with a railing a little wider than the tower.
- Lantern room with glazing, then a dark dome roof and a small finial.

## Focal hierarchy
1. Primary: the lantern room, with transparent glazing and a yellow beacon.
2. Secondary: the gallery railing and the tower's red bands.
3. Accents: a door at the base and small window bands.

## Palette roles
- Tower walls: white (colour 15).
- Bands: red (colour 4).
- Gallery, roof and lantern frame: dark grey (colour 72 or 0 as needed).
- Glazing: trans-clear (colour 47).
- Beacon: trans-yellow or yellow (colour 14).
- Plinth: tan or dark tan (colour 19 or 69) for rock.

## Depth
Each tower face is a SNOT module with studs pointing outward. Window bays are recessed one module, and the gallery projects past the tower.

## Connection strategy
The tower uses a SNOT core. Each face is built and checked as one module before it is repeated. The tower is generated by a Python script, which rotates the same tested module by 45-degree steps.

## Build order
1. Rock plinth (fixed, no SNOT).
2. Central support core.
3. Eight SNOT tower faces, with a connection test for the first face.
4. Gallery ring.
5. Lantern room and glazing.
6. Roof and finial.

## Scope
Static model. No moving parts. Physical validity is not proven.
