# Tidal Observatory - final visual review

Final MPD SHA-256: 4ac1340f73cbba19eaa69f3995dfde0e75427137bad11b5e197dc782aeaccccc

## Design outcome
Original 48 x 40-stud coastal institute, 2,734 physical placements in 20 embedded assembly sections. The broad two-storey hall and narrower tall lantern create the primary silhouette. A rounded sand-green copper roof contrasts with the lantern's dark-blue hipped cap. Limestone walls, white cornices, blue glazing frames and restrained brass fittings organize the palette. The approximate bounding size is 38.4 x 32.0 x 35.7 cm.

The front has three arched bays per level, a projecting entrance portico and shallow steps. Rear walls deliberately open into an exhibit cutaway. Furnishings include three chart desks with nautical printed tiles, archive shelves, chairs, desk lamps, inlaid floors and three static telescopes, one on the roof terrace. The quay includes two lamps, three planted boxes, a rear bench, low parapets and a tiled sea strip.

## Iterations after actually opening images
- V1 whole-model home/front/back and dome/window close-ups: the early paving used an unintended red color, the sides had excessive vertical masonry seams, the front needed depth, and the blue cap had poor corner shaping.
- V2 whole-model home/front/top and upper-interior top: corrected the stone palette to Dark Tan 28; replaced fragmented side-wall strips with longer bonded courses; added a real projecting portico; quieted the water pattern; added static brass telescopes.
- Final construction pass: tiled the accessible interiors with reserved furniture attachment footprints; gave the roof a quiet tiled terrace; cross-bonded the base plate layers; repaired cornice spacing and the clock ledge; removed tiles under the tower's rear footprint; reserved chair-back clearance; moved the rear planter away from the wall.
- Lantern cap close-up home/top: replaced stepped/missing corners with real 3045 double-convex hip slopes. The last close-up shows four continuous roof faces meeting at proper corners, with a small brass finial.

## Assessment against the brief and precedent
The low hall, rounded dome and tall glazed tower remain distinct at thumbnail size. The light cornices give useful shadows, while quiet tan side walls avoid indiscriminate ornament. The portico makes the entrance readable without blocking adjacent glazing. Rear openings expose the archive and chart room, rather than masquerading as a finished exterior facade.

This is not a recoloring of 5770 or Copper Lane. The lighthouse precedent informed only the contrast between a masonry tower, thin glass lantern and low foreground water. Copper Lane informed window-pane alignment, opening reservation and shelf techniques. The plan, footprint, storey proportions, portico, drum/copper roof, tower stages, harbor base and interior arrangement are new.

The original aspirational brief's working dome slit was deliberately replaced with a closed ornamental copper cap after the real-part study. The roof telescope supplies the visible scientific focal detail. No working dome rotation, shutter mechanism, internal staircase, lighting circuit or guaranteed minifigure circulation is claimed.

## Physical limits
No real bricks were assembled. Automated success and visual review do not certify strength, clutch, insertion access, glazing fit or retail color availability. In particular, prototype the round dome collar, studded telescope mounts, lamp collars, roof finials and window inserts before buying parts. See the verification record for each remaining warning.
